### Hello, world!

Recently, I've been talking to my friends a lot about philosophy, AI, and what to do in this ever-changing world.
There's pressure and uncertainty - if we believe with even 40% confidence that nothing will matter after [2028
(AGI)](https://blog.aifutures.org/p/q35-timelines-update-looks-like-the), the next two years would be so much more
important than the rest of our lives. Does this mean we only have **two years** to make a splash on the world? And many
(myself included) question if our work has any meaning on our journey through life.

As a first year college student, I sit in a very exciting position where I'm young enough to be an early adopter of AI
and in love with computers, but also old enough to seize this opportunity to do *something* with it that's hopefully
meaningful and not well done before. There's also so much uncertainty and conflicting viewpoints in the field - I can
read arguments with absolute conviction about how any sort of superintelligence would end humanity from Eliezer
Yudkowsky, or counterarguments that current AI development shows [no signs of an intelligence
explosion](https://www.hyperdimensional.co/p/iron-makes-iron). There's belief that our current financial systems and
power structures will be completely obliterated and overhauled, and everyone would be equal in face of
superintelligence.

Personally, I like to see the world through a top-down view. Consider first who you want to be and how you want to live,
and then how to get there. I believe in maximizing my life experiences for variety and *happiness*, and thus, any work I
do should contribute **utility** towards that variety and happiness.

But what brings variety and happiness? To me, it's the pursuit rooted deeply in our very human qualities - doing that
solo trip to china, trying the fanciest mochi dessert you've seen, reconnecting with old friends far away, and having
exciting problems to think about and work on. It's a way to live while preserving a spirit of exploration and escaping
the pit of a monotonous life.

In an ideal, world, I'd be able to align my work with variety and happiness and get all my birds with a single stone.
Unfortunately, my life isn't yet optimal. Perhaps this means my grade in my sociology class could be sacrificed to talk
with my research mentor about life/career advice (he said to make friends in college), or that I should spend my time on
passion projects with my friends instead of class (is this idea appalling?) I hope to escape the sunk cost fallacy of
*paying for college → must spend the next 4 years focused on classes*.

Then comes a fine balance between variety, happiness, and impact. You could have a high impact, exciting role with tons
of variety, but then struggle to find time for yourself or to pursue hobbies. Or maybe a high paying, high impact job
(hooray!) but it leaves you stagnant behind the same desk for 30 years. You could have a passion for mayan archaeology
and explore the past, but miss out on making an impact in the present.

And thus, I come back to the big question: does my work now have any meaning? (what should I do in the future?) - and my
answer now is that this uncertainty is what makes life *alive*. I hope that by living a life where I pursue as many
opportunities as possible and continuing to think critically, I'll end up with a good combination of happiness, variety,
and impact.

With this in mind, I believe that starting a blog has great utility for a handful of reasons:
 - It'll encourage me to continue [ *thinking luminously*
   ](https://www.lesswrong.com/s/ynMFrq9K5iNMfSZNg/p/9o3Cjjem7AbmmZfBs) and practice organizing my thoughts into a clear
   narrative
 - Hopefully others will be inspired to think critically about their lives and chase happiness, or learn from my journey
   (a net positive on the world!)
 - I can get feedback on my thinking and see other perspectives
 - Maybe someone will think I'm really cool

In this sense, I have a *hypothesis* about where to spend my time. No matter the result of this, there's a lot of value
in exploring hypotheses - and that's the point of this blog!
